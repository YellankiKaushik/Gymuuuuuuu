# Workout-science evidence governance

Draft identities are not articles. The supplied baseline bibliography does not substitute for claim-specific verification, and no baseline findings were copied into public factual content.

Publication requires independently reviewed definitions, three to six takeaways, decision context, atomic claims, exact references, studied populations, confidence, direction, limitations, and subject/editorial review. Quantitative guidance needs bounds, units, context, qualifiers and source IDs. Very-low evidence cannot become numeric guidance. Examples must explicitly be illustrative and non-personalized. Myths require sourced corrections and limitations.

Prefer current position stands, guidelines and research syntheses; preserve exact citation metadata. Practitioner frameworks are labelled as practice interpretation. Do not copy source prose, generalize clinical populations to healthy users without limits, promise precise outcomes, rank methods as universally best, duplicate exercise technique or publish automatically generated factual articles.

Review advanced methods and high-impact numeric guidance annually; stable definitions at least every two years. Display overdue review status and include it in the coverage report. Subject review must search newer evidence, consider conflicting findings and verify sources. Automated language checks are conservative; any universal-language exception needs the exact record field, source, reviewer and rationale in the separately reviewed exception file. Exceptions cannot authorize medical treatment claims.
